Ringolo vs typical calorie counters

Calorie counters log food in different ways. This page compares Ringolo with two common approaches: typing in every food by hand, and apps that log mainly by scanning barcodes. It describes how each one works rather than ranking them; which suits you depends on what you eat and how much time you want to spend.

Ringolo doesn't scan barcodes. For packaged food, search for it or enter the values from the label.

AI photo scans and voice logging are part of Ringolo Premium; new accounts can try photo scans in their first days.

What else Ringolo does

  • A daily ring that shows the calories left, with protein, carbs and fat against your own targets.
  • Logging by photo, voice, search or manual entry, with favorites and recent foods.
  • Food search with everyday foods and local dishes, in 15 languages.
  • Water tracking, and weight tracking with a goal line.
  • Meal, water and weekly weigh-in reminders.
  • Reports for a week, a month, 3 months or a year.
  • Home Screen and Lock Screen widgets.
  • An optional connection to Apple Health.
  • iPhone and iPad, in light or dark appearance.

Which approach fits you

  • Manual logging fits if you weigh your food and want every value to be exactly what you entered.
  • A barcode-based app fits if most of what you eat comes in a package.
  • Ringolo fits if you eat home-cooked or restaurant meals and want a quick estimate you can check, with a daily ring that shows what's left.

Whichever you use, nutrition values are estimates for general information, not medical advice. For counting calories from a photo step by step, see How to count calories from a photo.
